Fish

Fish are aquatic animals that breathe using gills instead of lungs, letting them pull oxygen straight from the water. They're vertebrates, meaning they have a backbone, and they use fins to steer and propel themselves.

There are more than 30,000 known species, found everywhere from mountain streams to the deepest parts of the ocean. Most fish are cold-blooded, so their body temperature shifts with their surroundings.

Shark

Sharks are a group of predatory fish with skeletons made of cartilage rather than bone, which makes them lighter and more flexible than bony fish. They've existed for over 400 million years, predating dinosaurs.

Most sharks rely on a keen sense of smell and the ability to detect electrical signals from other animals to hunt. Species range from the tiny dwarf lanternshark to the massive whale shark, the largest fish in the ocean.

Squid

Squid are soft-bodied cephalopods related to octopuses and cuttlefish. They have eight arms plus two longer tentacles, and most species can shoot out a cloud of ink to escape predators.

They move by jet propulsion, squeezing water out of their mantle to dart quickly through the ocean. Many squid can also change color almost instantly using special skin cells called chromatophores.

Dog

Dogs were domesticated from wolves tens of thousands of years ago, making them one of humanity's oldest animal companions. They're known for their loyalty, trainability, and keen sense of smell.

There are hundreds of recognized breeds, bred over centuries for tasks like herding, guarding, hunting, and companionship. Dogs communicate through body language, barks, and a wide range of facial expressions.

Cat

Cats were domesticated around 9,000 years ago, likely drawn to early farming settlements to hunt rodents. They remain more independent than most domestic animals, retaining strong hunting instincts.

Cats communicate through meows, purrs, body posture, and scent marking. They have excellent night vision and a flexible spine that lets them squeeze through tight spaces and land on their feet.

Hippo

Hippopotamuses are large semi-aquatic mammals native to sub-Saharan Africa. Despite their bulky build, they're excellent swimmers and spend much of the day submerged in rivers and lakes to stay cool.

Hippos are surprisingly fast on land, capable of short bursts of speed, and are considered one of the most dangerous animals in Africa due to their aggressive territorial behavior.
